SLIDE 2 — [1] The National AI Impact Programme is here.
Headline: A new national programme to make Singapore's workforce AI-fluent.
Key points:
* Singapore has launched the National AI Impact Programme (NAIIP) to train 100,000 workers to apply AI in their jobs by 2029.
* The programme also targets 10,000 enterprises to be supported in AI adoption over the next three years.
* It builds on Singapore's National AI Strategy 2.0 and is supported by the Ministry of Manpower and the Ministry of Education.
* Minister Teo described the vision as making workers "bilingual" — fluent in both their professional domain and AI — so they can solve real-world problems using technology.
Callout: This is not just for tech companies. It is designed for every industry in Singapore.
Source: Business Times, 2 Mar 2026. COS debate by Minister Josephine Teo (MDDI).


SLIDE 3 — [2] AI training is starting with accountancy and legal — and expanding fast.
Headline: Sector-specific AI fluency programmes are launching in the first half of 2026.
Key points:
* Accountants will learn to use AI to automate manual tasks like financial reporting and compliance monitoring, allowing them to work faster and more accurately.
* Lawyers will be trained in using AI for legal research, document review and contract management — freeing them to focus on analysis, strategy and client engagement.
* Software engineers will be upskilled to become full-stack developers capable of orchestrating complex systems and workflows powered by AI agents.
* IMDA is partnering with professional bodies including the Institute of Singapore Chartered Accountants (ISCA), the Singapore Academy of Law (SAL) and the Singapore Corporate Counsel Association (SCCA) to develop these programmes.
* Through TeSA programmes, participants will get hands-on experience with AI solutions and develop a stronger understanding of responsible AI usage, including AI bias and profession-specific risks.
Callout: You do not need to be in the tech industry to benefit. AI fluency is being built into everyday professional roles.
Source: Business Times, 2 Mar 2026. COS debate by Minister Josephine Teo (MDDI).


SLIDE 4 — [3] SMEs get pre-approved AI tools and grant support to start adopting.
Headline: The government is making it easier and cheaper for small businesses to start using AI.
Key points:
* Currently, only 15% of SMEs use AI — but about 7 in 10 workers already use AI in some way. The gap between enterprise adoption and individual usage shows there is untapped potential.
* IMDA and Enterprise Singapore have curated and pre-approved market-proven, cost-effective AI solutions from reputable vendors, so SMEs do not have to figure out which tools to trust.
* Grant support is available to reduce the cost of adopting these AI solutions, making it more affordable for businesses to take the first step.
* Minister Teo cautioned that if AI adoption mirrors previous technology waves, only a small group of frontier companies may reap disproportionate benefits — while smaller firms risk falling behind.
Callout: "We want to encourage those who haven't started to take the first step. Small businesses stand to gain the most from adopting AI." — Minister Josephine Teo
Source: Business Times, 2 Mar 2026. COS debate by Minister Josephine Teo (MDDI).


SLIDE 5 — [4] TeSA is expanding beyond tech — for the first time ever.
Headline: The TechSkills Accelerator programme is now reaching non-tech workers and sectors.
Key points:
* IMDA's TechSkills Accelerator (TeSA) was launched in 2016 and has placed more than 24,300 locals into tech domains like cybersecurity, cloud computing, AI and analytics.
* It has also upskilled over 440,000 individuals with tech skill sets since its launch — making it one of Singapore's largest workforce training programmes.
* For the first time, TeSA is expanding into non-tech occupations, starting with the accountancy and legal professions, which span multiple industries and have high exposure to AI applications.
* The goal is to equip workers in these sectors with the skills to transform their role-specific workflows using AI, with hands-on training and responsible AI practices built into the curriculum.
Callout: TeSA's expansion means AI training is no longer limited to tech workers. It is now available to professionals in every sector.
Source: Business Times, 2 Mar 2026. COS debate by Minister Josephine Teo (MDDI).
